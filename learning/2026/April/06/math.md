# Math: Scaling and Shifting Matrices

**1. Scalar Multiplication:**
A "scalar" is just a fancy mathematical term for a single, standard number (like $5$, or $-2.5$). When you multiply a matrix by a scalar, you simply multiply *every single element* inside the matrix by that number.

If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$, then $2A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 6 & 8 \end{bmatrix}$.

**2. Matrix Addition:**
To add two matrices together, you simply add the elements that are in the exact same positions. 


*Crucial Rule:* Because you match elements by position, you can **only** add matrices together if they have the exact same shape!

If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{bmatrix} 10 & 10 \\ 10 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$, then $A + B = \begin{bmatrix} 11 & 12 \\ 13 & 14 \end{bmatrix}$.
