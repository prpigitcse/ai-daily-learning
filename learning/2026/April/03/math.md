# Math: Finding the Minimum (Optimization)

Imagine you are blindfolded on the side of a bumpy, uneven mountain, and your goal is to reach the absolute lowest point in the valley. Because you are blindfolded, you cannot see the whole mountain to just walk directly to the bottom. 

How do you get down? You might tap your foot a few inches in a random direction. If the ground feels lower, you take a step. If it feels higher, you don't take that step, and you try a different direction. If you repeat this thousands of times, you will eventually reach the bottom.


In mathematics, this is called **Optimization**—the process of finding the minimum (or maximum) value of a function. The "mountain" is our Mean Squared Error (MSE) function, and the "bottom of the valley" is an error of 0. Right now, we are using a "random walk" approach to navigate this mathematical mountain. Later in our journey, we will learn Calculus (Gradient Descent), which acts like taking off the blindfold to calculate exactly which direction points the steepest way down.
